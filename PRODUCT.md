# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Prospective parents and guardians in west Paris, especially international, bilingual, relocating, and locally based families looking for an English-language education for children from early years through middle school. They arrive with high trust requirements: they need to understand whether the school is real, safe, caring, practical for their family, and transparent enough to contact or visit.

Secondary users include current families checking practical information, school staff keeping public information consistent, and search or AI systems evaluating whether Forest International School Paris is a credible local entity.

## Product Purpose

The website exists to make Forest International School Paris discoverable, credible, and easy to contact. It should show the real school in Mareil-Marly: its campus, teachers, nature-based learning, programmes, admissions path, fees, camps, and practical details.

Success means a parent can quickly see that the school is real, understand whether it fits their child, and take the next step through a visit, application, email, phone call, or WhatsApp contact. For content and SEO work, success also means search engines and AI assistants can identify the school as a specific west Paris international school rather than generic education content.

## Brand Personality

Warm, grounded, expert.

The voice should feel like a calm head of school speaking directly to one parent: concrete, human, British English, and specific to the school. It should be welcoming without sounding promotional, knowledgeable without sounding institutional, and nature-led without becoming decorative or whimsical.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic AI school marketing: vague benefits of bilingual education, abstract claims about nurturing potential, stock-sounding education copy, and unsupported superlatives.

Avoid template-heavy visual patterns: repeated identical card grids, pill labels above every section, oversized rounded cards, soft-shadow decoration, generic green nature branding, and design flourishes that do not help a parent make a decision.

Avoid content that risks trust or safety: invented facts, stale dates, inconsistent contact details, new photos of children without explicit approval, pupil personal data, or claims that cannot be traced to canonical school pages or confirmed by the user.

Do not make the site feel like a luxury school brochure, a corporate SaaS landing page, a playful children's brand, or a formal government institution. It should feel like this specific school: real, local, outdoors-connected, and careful with families' trust.

## Design Principles

Real proof over generic polish. Use concrete school evidence, actual campus imagery, real staff context, practical details, and first-hand local knowledge whenever the design needs to build confidence.

Parent decisions first. Structure pages around what families need to decide: age fit, curriculum, location, fees, admissions steps, safety, schedule, language, and how to visit or contact the school.

Trust before ornament. Visual choices should make facts easier to scan and the school easier to believe. Decoration, animation, cards, and badges only earn their place when they clarify hierarchy or add authentic school character.

Nature with restraint. The forest identity should come through real images, earthy colour discipline, and lived learning details, not generic leaves, icons, or decorative nature motifs.

Protect children and facts. Safeguarding, privacy, canonical facts, and production deployment rules override SEO and design ambition.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use WCAG AA as the baseline for future design work: readable contrast, visible focus states, keyboard-operable navigation and controls, meaningful alt text, semantic structure, and responsive layouts that keep text legible on mobile and desktop.

Motion must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Interactive elements, popups, menus, and contact actions should work clearly for keyboard users and should not depend on hover alone. Content should remain understandable for families reading in English as an additional language.
